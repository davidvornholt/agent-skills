# Soft print

## Essence

Soft print combines the typographic sophistication of magazine design with genuine warmth and welcome. It has editorial bones — strong hierarchy, confident serifs, considered layouts — wrapped in a generous, inviting temperature: cream paper tones, soft corners, gentle depth. It should feel like being received well: cared for, unhurried, personal.

## Typography

- Use an expressive, characterful serif for display type — one with warmth and slight eccentricity. Fraunces is the reference choice.
- Set body text in a readable, bookish serif. Newsreader is the reference choice; body copy in serif is part of the character.
- Editorial devices are welcome: drop caps on lead paragraphs, pull quotes as typographic statements, kickers with wide letterspacing.
- Hierarchy is classic magazine hierarchy: kicker, headline, deck, lead.

## Color

- The palette is warm paper: cream and parchment backgrounds, warm brown-leaning inks.
- Anchor on one earthy, food-warm accent. Terracotta is the reference choice; a golden secondary may support it.
- Color temperature is the single strongest lever of this style — warm it up before reaching for any other device.
- Never cool grays, never stark white.

## Shape and depth

- Corners are softly rounded — friendly, not bubbly. A moderate radius, consistent everywhere.
- Depth is soft and warm: low, diffuse shadows with a warm tint, as if lit by lamplight.
- Texture is welcome in small doses: a faint paper-grain overlay can carry the tactile quality.
- Layouts are generous — wide margins, roomy line lengths, unhurried spacing.

## Motion and interaction

- Motion is gentle and organic: soft eased transitions, slight lifts on hover, nothing abrupt.
- Interactions should feel considerate rather than snappy — ease over speed.

## Second mode (opt-in)

- This seed is light-first; it lives most naturally in light warmth. Build a dark variant only on explicit repo opt-in.
- If opted in, build it on deep warm browns rather than gray or black, and let the accent glow slightly warmer.

## Anti-patterns

- Do not drift into the staged lifestyle aesthetic: no washed-out, desaturated, decorative "Kinfolk" paleness. Warmth means color, not fading.
- Do not lose the editorial spine: without strong typographic hierarchy this collapses into a generic cozy template.
- No cool minimalism, no flat gray UI chrome, no sharp full-bleed black.
- Editorial devices are seasoning, not structure — one drop cap per page, not one per card.

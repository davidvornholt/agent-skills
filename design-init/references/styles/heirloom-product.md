# Heirloom product

## Essence

Heirloom product is warm, valuable, and enduring — the digital equivalent of a well-made heirloom. It pairs a noble serif with disciplined, sharp-edged construction, so the result reads as elegant and precise at the same time. It is emphatically not minimalism: surfaces are warm rather than white, color is present rather than withheld, and the design should feel furnished, not emptied.

## Typography

- Use a noble, high-contrast serif for display type — headings, statements, card titles. Spectral is the reference choice.
- Pair it with a quiet, competent grotesque for body and UI text. Hanken Grotesk is the reference choice.
- Build hierarchy through size and weight contrast between the two families; keep display tracking slightly tight.
- Set labels and metadata in the grotesque, small, letterspaced, uppercase.

## Color

- Anchor the palette in one primary accent with depth and dignity. Forest green is the reference choice.
- Ground it in warm neutrals — cream-leaning backgrounds, warm dark inks. Never pure white, never pure black.
- Allow a warm secondary accent for contrast moments, used sparingly.
- The overall temperature is warm; coolness is the fastest way to lose the character.

## Shape and depth

- Corners are square. 0px radius everywhere — buttons, cards, inputs, images.
- Perfect circles are the one shape exception: elements that are genuinely circular (width equals height) — radio controls, status dots, spinners, avatars — may be fully round. Print has always set circles beside its square blocks.
- Cards are explicitly allowed and encouraged; this style structures content, it does not flatten it.
- Use restrained depth: hairline borders and subtle shadows that suggest material without gloss. Reserve larger shadows for featured elements.
- Whitespace is generous but purposeful — roomy, never barren.

## Motion and interaction

- Motion is subtle and confident: short eased transitions, small lifts, no bounce, no spectacle.
- Micro-interactions must be best-in-class: hover, focus, and press states are deliberate design moments, not afterthoughts.

## Second mode (opt-in)

- This seed is light-first. Build a dark variant only on explicit repo opt-in.
- If opted in, dark must carry the same character, not an inversion: replace pure black with warm anthracite and keep inks warm off-white.
- Brighten the primary accent so it holds its dignity on dark ground — a lighter emerald in the reference palette.
- Trade shadows for slightly elevated surface tones; depth comes from lightness steps, not glow.

## Anti-patterns

- Do not drift into minimalism: no pale, washed-out palettes, no empty screens, no gray-on-white austerity.
- Do not drift into magazine styling: no drop caps, pull quotes, or print-production vocabulary as defaults — this is a product style, not an editorial layout.
- No rounded corners on rectangles, ever, even on small controls — and no pills: full rounding on anything wider than it is tall is a rounded corner, not a circle. Only true circles escape the square.
- No trend-driven effects: no glassmorphism, no gradients as decoration, no neon.
